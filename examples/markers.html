Marker series: shapes and clustering

Markers say "something is here", so their size is fixed: the moment size varies a reader starts decoding it as a quantity, and that is the bubble series. Clustering is an option on the same series rather than a separate one, because the data is identical and only the drawing changes. Clusters merge by real distance, not by grid cell, and sit at their members' centre of mass. Click one to fly to what is inside it, or zoom in and watch them dissolve.

Three decisions you can see

Distance, not cells. Bucketing points into grid cells is simpler, but it separates two points a few pixels apart that straddle a boundary while merging two at opposite corners of one cell. The grid here is only a neighbour index; the merge test is real distance. World space, quantized zoom. Panning never reclusters, so counts never renumber under the cursor, and a smooth pinch recomputes a handful of times instead of sixty times a second. Centre of mass, square-root sizing. A cluster drawn on its first member sits off to one side of the group it stands for. 

{
  type: 'marker',
  name: 'Sites',
  data: sites,               // { name, lon, lat, kind }
  shape: 'pin',              // circle square diamond triangle star cross pin
  size: 13,
  colorBy: 'kind',           // ordinal scale plus a legend, automatically
  cluster: { radius: 55, maxZoom: 6 },
}
